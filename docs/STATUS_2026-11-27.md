## How to reproduce verification

1. Inspect the public transactions on Devnet and the corresponding program-owned accounts.
2. With the operator's authorized, reviewed audit export, verify the ProofReceipt and ActionReceipt against the independently authenticated agent key; verify the controller-signed delegation and the attestation at the relevant execution time.
3. Recompute the batch, verify both inclusion proofs, and compare the expected commitment against finalized RPC state. See [Registry protocol](./PROOF_REGISTRY_DEVNET.md) and [Trust Fabric](./AGENT_TRUST_FABRIC_V0_1.md).

This page records observed results, not a standalone reproducible proof bundle for these two jobs. Raw prompts, session identifiers, private outputs and signing secrets are not published. Public transaction inspection independently establishes the on-chain commitment, not the contents of the private off-chain execution. The separate public [local test evidence pack](./evidence/devnet-registry-e2e-20261127.json) is reproducible with `node scripts/verify-devnet-registry-evidence.cjs` after building the repository; it uses a different, ephemeral identity.

## Acceptance boundaries

- **PASS:** two signed chat executions, binding and finalized Devnet commitments. Production frontend was deployed and returned HTTP 200 after PR #59; anonymous Durable status returned HTTP 401.
- **OPEN:** formal production browser disconnect/reopen acceptance; independent security audit; full cross-device persistence, distributed execution and high availability.
- **UNKNOWN:** production NeurOSA recall after service restart and live external search by Hermes. Controlled local tests are not evidence of those capabilities.
- **NOT CLAIMED:** Mainnet deployment, hardware-backed attestation, domain-verified agent identity, correctness of arbitrary AI responses or enterprise GA. The original ProofReceipt V2 signature field remains UNAVAILABLE; it is the separate ActionReceipt that is signed. Runtime attestation is software-declared and signed, not TEE-backed. Registry anchors commitments; it does not verify inference on-chain.

No claim of a third-party security certification or global uniqueness is made.
